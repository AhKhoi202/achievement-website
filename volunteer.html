CHAPTER 3

VOLUNTEER

Founder, Mentorion - VN25

In my 9th-grade life, my dad always urged me to do something meaningful for society. Coincidentally, after a GCED lesson about researching local children who have been suffering from poor English education, I was befuddled and surprised at myself for not knowing about it. As a local with quite decent English literacy, I decided to have a first kick-off. I worked on the latest fundamental model in April 2025, when I had only 13 members who were my old teammates in the project, family members, best friends, and classmates. As a founder, I had to solve the question of an efficient model and workplace style to gather my teachers and learners together. After a month of overthinking, I finally launched the project "Give You A Dream" - changed into "MENTORION" in June 2025, and GYAD became our fund - for the first time with my money, "A Day as a CEO"'s prize. Due to my poor experience in teaching and class management, the first lesson wasn't phenomenal for the kids. Day by day, I grew with feedback from my friends and the students, as I love to listen and improve my ability to deliver knowledge in a better way. Until now, thanks to Mr. Vu Tran Tuan Anh, my dearest partner in management, whom I recruited after the first month and who initially stayed in the teaching department, our team now has 20+ members with an outstanding profile of teachers who have 7.0 to 8.0 IELTS. Moreover, our method has been changing to provide the best learning experience for orphans and ADHD kids at Que Huong Shelter in my hometown, Di An.

Đi thiện nguyện mùa lũ

My hometown is in the Central Area of Vietnam, which was hit hard by historic storms and floods in 2025, causing $3.96 billion in damages across the country. Hearing the news, my dad's company launched a charity drive to provide flood relief. Coincidentally, I had just landed back in Vietnam a week before I decided to join the trip. On our way, we traveled from Ho Chi Minh City to Binh Thuan and Phu Yen provinces, areas that had suffered severe damage to people and livelihoods, especially affecting the elderly. During this journey, I heard about the pitiful cases of the victims and the irresponsibility of several local personnel regarding the dam that was supposed to prevent the water. When I went to some villages, the trace of muddy flood still existed on the wall and left behind tears and sorrow for the children and families who lost their relatives, parents, grandparents, and siblings. Every family used to have vibrant light; now it turns into the flickering yellow light of burning incense and mournful, bleak worship. We went around and addressed some struggling families to give some decent contributions to help their families overcome the situation. People actually burst into tears and retold us their entire story and the grief of losing their members or the freaking moments to flee from the sudden flow of water that burst into their house. Someone had lost their entire property and got into debt if they are fishers or rearing lobsters in Phu Yen because the river water blends into the salt water in the bay. This trip brought me lots of sense about life and the hardships as the destiny for any Central local.

Thiện nguyện đi cùng AS

During my summer break of 2026, I decided to open my eyes to the South, where I have never been in education duty. I taught more than 58+ children from grades 1 to 5 at Tan Thuy Primary School about technology and English. With my initial experience in MENTORION, approaching these pupils became easier for me than my first time staying in role. Here, I led a program of dancing to say goodbye to the students and an act to raise awareness of technological scams. I came up with the idea of doing a fun game that digitally functions to have the students interact with multiple resources and materials. Over the entire 16 days, we did a lot of bonding activities like STEM booths, sports events, and fixing school property. Although I was exhausted after over 14 -16 hours of constantly working and preparing for events and lessons, I still felt really proud of my students' studiousness and their curiosity.
